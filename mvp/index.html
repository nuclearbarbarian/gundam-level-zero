The Case

Gundam Level Zero uses the construction of a Heavyarms-class Gundam — a 20–40-ton bipedal combat platform after Trowa Barton's Heavyarms Custom — as a diagnostic benchmark on the U.S. and allied industrial and innovation base. Heavyarms is treated as a design brief, not a frame-perfect blueprint.

Heavyarms is interesting precisely because it would trigger redline effort across almost every industrial domain at once: heavy actuators, frame metallurgy at combat tonnage, bipedal locomotion at scale, sensor fusion under EW, integrated armament, multinational specialty-alloy supply, and a doctrinal case for fielding it. Asking what would have to be true for the United States and its allies to build one casts industrial-base problems in stark relief that procurement-line analysis tends to flatten.

The project is diagnostic — what the Heavyarms exposes — and programmatic — the roadmap to reach the threshold at which it becomes possible to build one.

The argument is American-led with allied industrial integration. Baseline partners are Korea, Japan, Germany, Taiwan, the Netherlands, and Switzerland. AUKUS §1080 (NDAA FY24) and DPA Title III extend the partnership to the United Kingdom, Australia, and Canada. The People's Republic of China is firmly excluded per NSIBR (15 C.F.R. 700–709), DFARS Case 2021-D015, and the BIS §232 surface. Gundam Level Zero is the threshold at which the allied industrial system can produce a Heavyarms-class platform for combat use. Not a procurement proposal. A benchmark.

Project framing per CLAUDE.md and ADR 0002 (2026-04-20). Primary audience: defense analysts (DARC paper commission). Secondary: general public via the three-layer static site (The Case / The Spec / Gundam Level Zero) presently under construction.
